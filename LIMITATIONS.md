# Evidence and inference limits

This project intentionally uses narrow, source-bounded language.

1. **Purposive selection.** The 12 diagnostic examples include cases selected after problems were suspected; they cannot estimate an outlet's or population's error rate.
2. **Separate sampling frame.** The ten-slot outlet/event grid was selected retrospectively, is not a preregistered sample, and includes a dynamic live blog and a not-located slot. Four article URLs overlap across the collections.
3. **Source versus real-world outcome.** An SSC corrigendum establishes what SSC publicly asserted, not independent roster-level truth. NTA refund notices are not bank settlement records. A CUET rescheduling notice is not proof a resit occurred.
4. **Different verdicts.** A selected SSC article **contradicts the agency's explicit statement**. Selected NEET claims of completed refunds are **not established by the cited notice**; this does not prove that refunds never occurred.
5. **Time and archives.** A web page's current content does not authenticate its text on the first publication date; no independently preserved publication-day snapshot corpus is provided.
6. **AI-assisted single-workflow labels.** No external human second-coder, blinded adjudication or inter-rater reliability study.
7. **No newsroom causality.** The project cannot establish a reporter's motive, reliance on other outlets, independence of similar articles or effects on candidates.
8. **No new algorithmic invention.** W3C standards and prior research already cover provenance, data revisions and validation. This project is a documented applied audit, not a validated model that outperforms standard source-checking.
9. **No production deployment or human study.** The verifier checks local CSV structure, not actual news accuracy, government outcomes or cognitive processes.
10. **Publication status.** The report is a working draft; it is not peer-reviewed or formally accepted.

## Appropriate scope of the contribution

The study demonstrates a reproducible **question-asking and coding structure**: who published a claim, what exact administrative object it concerns, at which stage, in which period and population, and whether the identified notice establishes, denies or leaves the reported proposition unresolved.

The audit could be challenged, extended or independently replicated using frozen page archives, prespecified sampling, independent coding and standards-based baselines.
